## Introduction

On 31 July 2026, the Advance Decision on Life-sustaining Treatment Ordinance took effect, finally giving Advance Medical Directives (AMD) full statutory protection in Hong Kong. But when the law came into force, many people already had an AMD — signed in 2024 or 2025 using the Hospital Authority's old form.

Their first question: "Is my old form still valid?"

The short answer: **Yes. You don't need to sign a new one.**

The Ordinance contains a "grandfathering" clause that protects AMDs signed before the law took effect. This article explains which old AMDs are valid, what to check, and how to make sure healthcare staff actually "know" about your AMD.

## What is the "grandfathering" clause?

"Grandfathering" means: documents that already existed before a new law took effect remain recognised, as long as they meet the specified conditions. They don't suddenly become invalid.

The Ordinance's arrangement: **AMDs signed before commencement using the Hospital Authority (HA) form are automatically grandfathered and remain valid.**

This makes sense — people who planned ahead shouldn't be penalised because the government legislated late. Your wishes don't carry less weight just because your form is an older version.

## Which old AMDs are definitely valid?

Two situations:

**① Hospital Authority (HA) forms**
If you signed before commencement, it's automatically valid. This is the safest case.

**② Non-HA formats**
If you didn't use the HA form (e.g. you wrote your own or used another organisation's format), it's still respected as long as the signing requirements were met at the time. See [Part 2 of Schedule 1 of the Ordinance](https://www.elegislation.gov.hk/hk/cap651!en) for details.

In other words, the law looks at whether the **procedures were followed when you signed** — not where the paper came from.

## No need to re-sign — but check two things

Your old AMD is valid, but that doesn't mean you can ignore it. We recommend digging out your old document and checking:

**① Witness requirements**
- Were there two witnesses when you signed?
- Was one of them a registered medical practitioner?
- Were both witnesses free of any conflict of interest (not beneficiaries of your estate)?

**② Content requirements**
- Does it clearly specify one of the three qualifying conditions (terminal illness / persistent vegetative state or irreversible coma / other advanced irreversible life-limiting condition)?
- Does it specify which life-sustaining treatments you refuse (CPR, ventilator, dialysis, etc.)?

If it was signed hastily, the conditions aren't complete, or the document is damaged — consider re-executing a new AMD for peace of mind.

## How do healthcare staff actually "know" about your AMD?

This is a point many people miss. The Ordinance sets a clear standard for "notice" — healthcare staff must either:

- **See a validating copy of your AMD** (the original or a certified copy), or
- **Be informed that a copy is or may be stored in the designated electronic system (eHealth), and an accessible copy is in fact stored there**

In other words, signing an AMD and keeping it in a drawer isn't enough. If you collapse on the street, paramedics have no way to know your AMD exists.

**Practical advice:**
1. Carry a copy with you (or leave one with a trusted family member)
2. Upload it to eHealth (in Phase 1, signed paper AMDs can be voluntarily uploaded with a registered doctor's assistance)
3. Tell your family where the AMD is kept

## FAQ

**Q1: My old form isn't an HA format — is it still valid?**
A: Yes, if the conditions are met. As long as you had two witnesses, one being a registered doctor, with no conflict of interest, a non-HA format AMD is still respected (Part 2 of Schedule 1 of the Ordinance).

**Q2: I signed an old AMD but want to change it — what do I do?**
A: Simply execute a new AMD. When you make a new one, the old one is automatically deemed revoked. So if you want to change your wishes, sign a new one — no need to separately revoke the old document.

**Q3: Does my old form need to be uploaded to eHealth to be valid?**
A: No. Being "valid" and being "known to healthcare staff" are two different things. Your AMD is valid without uploading; but uploading makes it easier for staff to find it when it matters.

**Q4: I've never signed one — which form should I use now?**
A: Use the new statutory standard form (HA / Health Bureau). You need a registered doctor to witness and explain the document — the process takes about 30 minutes. That's exactly what DoctorNow's home-visit service provides.
